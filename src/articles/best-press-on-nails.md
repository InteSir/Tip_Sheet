  - q: "Glue or adhesive tabs: which is better?"
    a: Nail glue usually holds longer, often around one to two weeks with good prep. Adhesive tabs are gentler and easier to remove, and suit wearing a set for a few days or an event. Many kits include both.
  - q: Can press-on nails be reused?
    a: Often, yes, especially if you used adhesive tabs. Remove them gently, clean off leftover adhesive and store them in their original tray.

Press-on nails have come a long way. The best sets now look like a salon manicure, last well with good prep, and cost far less. The trick is knowing what to look for.

There's no single best press-on brand. Whether a set works for you depends mostly on three things: how curved or flat your nail beds are, whether you use glue or adhesive tabs, and how long you want to wear it. The same set can hold well on one person and lift quickly on someone with flatter or more curved nails. So instead of a ranking, this guide shows you how to judge any set for yourself.

## How to choose press-on nails

<table>
  <thead><tr><th>Consider</th><th>What it means for you</th></tr></thead>
  <tbody>
    <tr><td>Shape</td><td>Almond and oval lengthen fingers; squoval and round are the most practical; square and coffin show off nail art.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Length</td><td>Short is easiest for typing and everyday tasks. Medium gives room for detail. Long is for statement looks.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Finish</td><td>Glossy is classic, chrome and cat eye add shine, and matte looks modern but shows wear sooner.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Fit</td><td>More sizes in the box means a better fit. A set with 24 or more nails usually covers most hands.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Curve</td><td>Width isn't everything. Tips that are flatter or more curved than your nail beds lift at the sides or press and hurt, so check the curve too.</td></tr>
    <tr><td>Adhesive</td><td>Glue for longer wear, adhesive tabs for a few days or an event.</td></tr>

</table>
