When a press-on set fails early, it's tempting to blame the glue or the brand. But when you read through community threads, brand help pages and nail-artist tutorials, the same story keeps coming up: **most failures come down to prep and fit, not the glue.** Oil left on the nail, a tip that's slightly too wide, a blob of glue pooling at the cuticle, a sink full of dishes an hour after applying: small things decide whether a set lasts two days or two weeks.

This hub is built to help you work out what went wrong. Find the symptom that sounds like yours below, check the likely causes, and try the fixes in order. Where pros and brands disagree, we say so and explain why, rather than picking a winner without evidence.

A quick note on expectations: with glue and careful prep, wearers commonly report about **one to two weeks**. Adhesive tabs are made for shorter wear, typically **a few hours to about three days**. Box claims often run longer than both. Your own results will vary with your nail shape, your set and how much your hands are in water.

## Diagnose by where it fails

Look at the nail that failed. Where it let go is the best clue to why.

| Where it fails | Likely cause | Go to fix |
|---|---|---|
| Whole tip pops off clean, day 1–2 | Prep: oil, shine or moisture left on the nail; water too soon | [Pops off in a day or two](#pop-off-in-days) |
| Lifts at the cuticle | Glue pooled at the base, cuticle not pushed back, tip touching skin | [Lifting at the cuticle or sides](#lifting-cuticle-sides) |
| Lifts along one side | Oil in the side groove, or the tip overhangs the sidewall | [Lifting at the cuticle or sides](#lifting-cuticle-sides) |
| Gap at the base or sides from the start | Curve or width mismatch, or a pre-applied adhesive ledge | [Gaps at the cuticle or sides](#gaps-cuticle-sides) |
| Pressure or pain along the sides | Tip too wide (sits on skin) or too narrow (squeezes) | [Sore or pressing sides](#sore-sides) |
| Springs up soon after pressing down | Tip too small and forced flat | [Between two sizes](#between-sizes) |
| Bubbles or a visible glue pattern | Tip pressed flat, uneven glue, sheer tip | [Bubbles or visible glue](#bubbles-visible-glue) |
| Nail looks thin or patchy after removal | Tips forced off before the glue softened | [Damage on removal](#removal-damage) |

If several nails fail in the same place, the cause is almost always the routine. If it's just one finger (often a pinkie or thumb), it's usually that finger's fit.

## The three habits that fix most problems

You'll see these repeated across most of the fixes below, because they come up again and again in the advice:

1. **Prep every nail the same way.** Push back the cuticle, buff off the shine, wipe with alcohol, then keep your fingers off the nail.
2. **Size before you glue.** Lay out a tip for every finger first. Each one should cover your nail sidewall to sidewall without touching skin.
3. **Less glue, more pressure.** A thin, even layer, pressed on at an angle and held firmly, beats a big drop.

Some wearers in press-on communities also lightly rough up the inside of the press-on before gluing, and one glue brand lists etching the inside of the tip as an expert tip. It's optional; if you try it, keep it light.

<div class="callout callout--safety"><p><strong>Safety first.</strong> Nail glue is a cyanoacrylate: it bonds skin, so keep it off your cuticles and fingers. Some people react to adhesives. If you get itching, redness, swelling, blisters or a rash (including away from your nails, such as on your eyelids or face; on darker skin a rash may look dark, purplish or gray rather than red), stop using the product, remove the set gently and see a doctor or dermatologist. Allergy to acrylates (the chemicals in gel products and gel glues) is usually lifelong once it develops, and “HEMA-free” does not mean acrylate-free. UV/LED-cured gel glue may not cure fully under opaque press-on tips, because the lamp's light can't reach it. This page shares general information, not medical advice.</p></div>
