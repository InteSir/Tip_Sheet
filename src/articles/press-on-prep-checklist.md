Ask experienced press-on wearers why a set lasted two days instead of two weeks, and the answer is rarely the glue. It's usually the ten minutes before it: a bit of oil left on the nail, a cuticle that wasn't pushed back, a tip that was slightly too wide, or a hand in the sink too soon. The same prep steps come up again and again across brand instructions, nail-artist tutorials and community threads.

This checklist puts them in order. Print it, keep it with your kit, and tick through it every time. With glue and good prep, wearers commonly report about one to two weeks of wear; tabs are made for a few hours to about three days. Results vary, but skipping steps almost always costs you days.

## A few notes on the steps

**Buff lightly.** The goal is to take the shine off so the adhesive can grip, not to thin your nail. Over-filing and forcing tips off are what leave nails thin and sore.

**About roughing up the inside of the tip.** This one comes from the community: wearers in press-on groups often list sanding or buffing the inside of the press-on among their fixes, and one glue brand includes etching the inside of the tip in its expert tips. Treat it as optional and keep it light.

<div class="callout"><p><strong>Why advice conflicts:</strong> brands give different no-water windows (roughly one to two hours for glue, three to four hours for tabs), and sources disagree on whether to file press-ons before or after applying. A tab-plus-glue "hybrid method" is popular online but described by only one brand source, which also cautions about trapped moisture, so we haven't included it here. When your box gives instructions, follow them.</p></div>

<div class="callout callout--safety"><p><strong>Safety first.</strong> Nail glue is a cyanoacrylate and bonds skin, so keep it off your cuticles and fingers. Some people react to adhesives. UV/LED-cured gel glue may not cure fully under opaque press-on tips, because the lamp's light can't reach it, which increases the risk of allergy. Allergy to acrylates is usually lifelong once it develops, and “HEMA-free” does not mean acrylate-free. If you notice itching, redness, swelling or a rash, stop, remove the set gently and see a doctor or dermatologist. This is general information, not medical advice.</p></div>
